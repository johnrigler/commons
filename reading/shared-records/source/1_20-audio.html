A conversation can become a source record

Preserve the recording, check the transcript, and connect the relevant passage to the question it helps answer.

Label what the recording actually contains

A voice note can preserve a person's account soon after an event. A recorded interaction can preserve what was said, including a menu, transfer, request, or response. A demonstration or role-play can illustrate a mechanism. Label these different sources accurately so someone reviewing the record knows what each one supports.

Capture can begin on a phone. Give the original file a source label and add a short account of the participants as known, the purpose of the interaction, and the question at issue. Mark unknown identities or dates as unknown.

Keep the original and the chronology

Retain the original exported file privately without trimming, normalizing, or replacing it. Record its format, duration, SHA-256 digest, provider, and intake date. Preserve capture metadata when available, with its time zone and any uncertainty. The claimed event time, the time the file entered the case archive, and any later blockchain anchor are separate observations.

Give a transcript, redacted recording, or compilation its own identity and link it to the source. Each excerpt should identify the source passage it uses. Preserve the private edit history so later reviewers can understand how a public derivative was made. NIST's preservation guidance supports documenting sources and using hashes to check file integrity.[1]

Use AI to reduce the work of review

Produce a transcript with time references and provisional speaker labels. Check it against the recording, especially names, amounts, dates, and statements attributed to another person. Mark unclear speech and uncertainty. Preserve a corrected transcript as another edition.

An assistant can connect a passage to a dated event, related letter, or unresolved request. A voice note remains the contributor's account; automatic transcription does not turn it into independent confirmation. A summary should let its reader return to the relevant source passage.

Choose what becomes public

Keep unreviewed source audio in the private case record. Review the recording and transcript independently for account information, verification answers, personal details, and material outside the intended public account. A redacted audio export and its transcript need separate checks.

Before recording or publishing a conversation with others, establish the relevant permissions and privacy rules for the people and jurisdictions involved. The shared law index and the call-recording design note retain the existing recording-law distinctions. A company address alone does not establish where a participant is located.

Connect the passage to the shared question

A case entry can identify what the caller requested, what answer was given, which handoffs occurred, and what remained unanswered. Keep other documents and subsequent answers connected to that event. This makes the effort of seeking a response visible without assuming every delay has the same cause.

A reviewed public excerpt may help another contributor recognize a similar process. Compare independent source records, preserve differences between cases, and give later responses and corrections clear references. The shared-accountability argument explains how that comparison can support collective scrutiny.

This is a proposed workflow, not an audio upload or editing service. The current public artifact tool handles PDFs and correspondence. The planned audio record separates original, transcript, public derivative, signature, publication, and ledger receipt; each is recorded only when it actually exists.
