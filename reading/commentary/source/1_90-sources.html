Opinion, observations, and published rules

The judgment is John Rigler's. Public sources supply the legal framework and each company's published description.

  1. Office of the Law Revision Counsel, 15 U.S.C. §1681i. Current text through 7 October 2026. Reinvestigation and relevant information, results and procedure notices, and the distinct statement-of-dispute provisions in subsections (b)–(c). The latter permit a clear and accurate codification or summary; a downloadable verbatim submission history is a proposed design standard here, not a quoted statutory requirement.
  2. Experian, Dispute Credit Report Information. Public instructions for adding a statement and supplying supporting documents. These do not independently reproduce the authenticated interface route described by Rigler.
  3. TransUnion, How to Read Your Credit Report, Consumer Statement section. Describes prepared statements and writing one's own; relevant counterexample to a universal canned-menu claim.
  4. National Credit Systems, About NCS. The company's property-collection model and account-service options.
  5. Rausch Sturm LLP, official website and consumer FAQ. Creditor representation, the Resolution Specialist Team, portal, and consumer contact routes. It does not independently establish a pattern of delaying lawyer access.
  6. Midland Credit Management, FAQs. The company's descriptions of account ownership, servicing, reporting, and resolution.
  7. FTC, What To Do if a Debt Collector Sues You. Responding to court papers and the distinction between the collector's claim and its responsibility to prove it.
  8. Federal Rules of Appellate Procedure with Fifth Circuit Rules, amended through 1 December 2025, Rule 29. An example of court-specific amicus requirements; not a universal trial-court filing procedure.
