PTSD: assessment, evidence, and benefits

Clinical diagnosis, Social Security disability, and VA compensation follow different standards.

  1. National Institute of Mental Health, Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder. Civilian and military experiences, symptom groups, clinical assessment, and duration of symptoms.
  2. SSA, SSI Eligibility, 2026 edition. Adult and childhood disability definitions, duration, financial conditions, and other requirements.
  3. SSA, How Does Someone Become Eligible?. SSDI insurance/work conditions and the sequential disability evaluation, including work-related steps after the listings.
  4. SSA, Adult Mental Disorders, listing 12.15 and sections 12.00C–G. Required medical findings, the alternative A+B and A+C routes, functional severity, sustained functioning, support, and marginal adjustment.
  5. SSA, Evidentiary Requirements. Establishing a medically determinable impairment and evaluating severity with medical and nonmedical evidence.
  6. SSA, SSR 16-3p, Evaluation of Symptoms in Disability Claims. Effective 28 March 2016, revised in 2017; symptom evaluation and consideration of reasons for limited treatment. Rules addressing failure to follow prescribed treatment are a separate issue.
  7. SSA, Medical Evidence. Do not delay filing for records; agency evidence requests and possible examinations at agency expense.
  8. SSA, Information for Military & Veterans: SSDI and VA Disability comparison. Different definitions, military-service conditions, work standards, and separate applications.
  9. VA, Disability Compensation for PTSD. PTSD diagnosis and connection to an in-service traumatic event; specific eligibility and claim requirements.
  10. VA, About Disability Ratings. The rating system, evidence, examinations, and compensation rates.

The discussion of application burden and the proposed organization of functional evidence are editorial analysis. Examples of work effects illustrate documentation questions; they do not diagnose PTSD or predict a benefit award. This is an adult-focused explanation of selected federal standards, with links to the existing New Mexico/Texas series. No independent clinical or legal review is recorded.
