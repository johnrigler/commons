## The question this document answers

Can a person make a legally meaningful written dispute before hiring a lawyer or opening a lawsuit? Yes. A qualifying written notice to a covered third-party collector can trigger Texas Finance Code section 392.202 before court proceedings. Certified delivery helps establish receipt. It is not a special phrase or postal service that substitutes for the required notice and coverage.

A person can clearly dispute a balance even though the recipient declines to correct it. An unfavorable response does not, by itself, show that the notice was incorrectly written. A request for the balance after mitigation and credits should not be silently converted into identity theft, hardship or payment negotiations.

## Two issues to preserve separately

The first issue is whether the underlying balance and the credit-report entry, often called a tradeline, are accurate. The second is whether each responsible organization met the duties triggered by the written dispute and any bureau-transmitted notice. Correction of the amount and liability for a proven earlier dispute-handling failure are separate questions.

A complaint can therefore examine both the accounting and what happened after notice: the actual investigation, consideration of the stated objection, any required collection pause, the statement or results supplied, and required corrections or notifications. Which duties apply depends on the actor and notice route. A landlord letter, collector letter and bureau dispute are not interchangeable triggers.

A later correction does not automatically make an earlier violation timely or eliminate an otherwise viable claim about past conduct. Sending a letter does not automatically create a second violation, however. Identify the specific duty, evidence of breach, each person's claim and the available remedy. A timely denial is not conclusive evidence of a proper investigation; failure to provide every requested document is not conclusive evidence of an improper one.

## What starts the Texas clock

Section 392.202(a) addresses a written notice disputing the accuracy of an item in the relevant collector or credit-bureau file relating to a debt being collected by the third-party collector. Identify the disputed item and explain the asserted inaccuracy. The statute does not require a lawyer, a pending case, certified mailing or a particular citation in the notice. Naming the statute can make the request clearer; it does not create coverage.

Receipt by the covered collector is the relevant event for the subsection (b) statement deadline. A notice sent only to the source creditor, owner or manager does not automatically establish receipt by that collector. A source party might have other duties; being the landlord or manager does not automatically make section 392.202 applicable to it.

The statute distinguishes reporting and nonreporting collectors in subsection (a), requires a written record of the dispute, and provides collection-cessation and investigation conditions. For a collector that initiates an investigation, subsection (b) requires a written statement no later than the 30th day after receipt. That statement must admit the inaccuracy, deny it, or state that there has not been sufficient time to finish investigating.

## What the clock does not establish by itself

The thirty-day statement requirement is not a general right to receive every requested lease, ledger, marketing record or replacement lease within thirty days. Those documents can be important to the merits and investigation; a private request does not turn them into court-ordered production.

A timely denial can satisfy the statement requirement while leaving other applicable duties unresolved. Its existence does not establish that an actual investigation addressed the disputed item. Conversely, unanswered document requests do not alone prove that the collector failed to investigate.

If the collector states it has not had sufficient investigation time, subsection (d) requires immediate changes, recipient notifications and cessation as specified there. That is different from denying an inaccuracy because the collector says the consumer has not substantiated it. Do not treat those two statements as interchangeable.

Under subsection (e), the collector must report the completed determination to the individual; a determination of accuracy can permit renewed reporting and collection. A real earlier violation is not made timely by later compliance. Delay does not automatically extinguish an otherwise valid debt or exclude every later record.

## Rental accounting and the investigation question

A spreadsheet can explain how posted numbers add up without establishing the authority or facts behind them. Separate the lease clause, trigger, charged period, mitigation efforts, availability, replacement tenancy and credits.

A statement that the creditor previously validated the debt does not, by itself, establish when that review happened, what it covered, or whether the written mitigation objection was considered afterward. Those are questions to establish from evidence, not grounds to assume either a genuine review or the absence of one.

Ask what the collector requested from the source party, what that party supplied, who reviewed it and when, and how the final balance was determined. Ask the property-side custodian for unit-specific availability, reletting and adjustment records. A missing standalone report does not alone prove that reasonable mitigation efforts never occurred.

## Why this remains a sludge problem

A person may identify the issue clearly, retain proof of delivery and still receive a referral or a denial without the explanation sought. The work needed to find the responsible actor, distinguish legal duties and obtain the underlying records is administrative burden. It can discourage a challenge without proof of a deliberate plan.

The existence of that burden does not make the consumer's original notice meaningless. Court process may become necessary to enforce an existing duty or obtain evidence. It does not create all duties for the first time, and a private letter is not itself court discovery.

## Separate federal and court procedures

The federal FDCPA validation window generally concerns when the consumer submits a written dispute after receiving validation information; it is not a fixed thirty-day deadline for the collector to deliver every source document. Bureau and furnisher investigations have different recipients, notice triggers and enforcement rules. Do not combine those clocks with the Texas procedure.

Before filing, identify a supportable cause of action, responsible entity, remedy, jurisdiction, venue and filing deadline.
