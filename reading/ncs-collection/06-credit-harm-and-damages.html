06 · Proving impact

Credit Reporting Injury and Potential Remedies

A disputed collection on a bureau report can be consequential. A claim for money still requires a valid statutory theory and, for many forms of relief, evidence of concrete injury and causation.

Separate the questions

  1. Was the reported collection or amount materially inaccurate or incomplete?
  2. What statutory duty was triggered and what did NCS or another furnisher actually do?
  3. Was it furnished or disseminated, and to whom?
  4. What actual financial, reputational or otherwise legally cognizable harm resulted?
  5. How can that harm be quantified without assuming every score change came from this one collection?

What to preserve now

  • Historical scores: export any visible graph or table with date, credit bureau, model (FICO/VantageScore) and score; browser print-to-PDF where no native export exists. Do not mix scoring models as if directly comparable.
  • Complete bureau disclosures: request/save Experian, Equifax and TransUnion reports from AnnualCreditReport.com. A free credit report is not the same thing as a credit-score history.
  • Dispute results: preserve each prior bureau decision, submission date and NCS tradeline as reported. The author reports deletion by two bureaus and continued Experian reporting, but the exact dates and independent source documents are to be verified.
  • Recipients and decisions: retain third-party credit inquiries, landlord-screening records, loan adverse-action or risk-based pricing notices, rate/fee differences and application outcomes. Do not seek unnecessary new credit applications to manufacture an adverse action.
  • Investigation records: request an appropriate FCRA §1681g file disclosure and, for completed bureau reinvestigations, a description of procedures under §1681i(a)(7). Historical scores are not always required parts of a §1681g file disclosure.

Possible damages, not an estimate

ClaimPotential remedyImportant limit
FDCPA, §1692kActual damages and up to $1,000 additional statutory damages in an individual action; fees/costs if successful.$1,000 is generally per individual action against a defendant, not per call or letter.
Texas §392.403Actual damages/injunction; fees and costs for successful claims; at least $100 per violation for specified provisions including §392.202.Statutory minimum does not apply indiscriminately to every section or every alleged procedural inconvenience.
FCRA negligent conduct, §1681oActual damages, fees/costs when available.Requires actionable violation and damages caused by it.
FCRA willful conduct, §1681nActual or $100–$1,000 statutory damages, possible punitive damages, fees/costs.Willfulness, privately enforceable duty and applicable standing requirements must be established.
Underlying rental chargeDefense/reduction of unsupported amount; potentially other proper relief.Eliminating an unpaid $10,365.56 obligation does not itself mean a $10,365.56 cash award.

A reduction in a score may support a causal narrative but is not automatically a dollar loss per point. Third-party disclosure, denial, higher deposit or interest, supported distress, and similar harms require their own documentary analysis. Claims may overlap and cannot simply be stacked to double-recover the same harm. Government regulatory fines, where available, are not paid to the consumer.

Exact-edition evidence

A date-stamped credit score screenshot obtained today may depict a score from months ago. Preserve both the historical observation date and the retrieval date. Hash original downloads without altering them; give generated charts, redactions and final signed editions separate identifiers. No score history or third-party rejection record is included in this public article.
