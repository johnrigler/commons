08 · Questions that can be answered

Next Call and Document Requests

Do not repeat a pending dispute solely to be told the response clock has not expired. The immediate research question is who can perform substantive review and what evidence could change the result.

The status constraint

In the November 4 conversation, an NCS collection manager described a new dispute received October 26 and said the property had up to 30 days to respond. Thirty calendar days after October 26 is November 25, 2026, but this is not a conclusion that every legal investigation deadline is November 25. The governing notice, receipt, statute, and any earlier separate disputes must be identified. The author expects a routine “wait for the response” explanation if the next call happens earlier.

Short procedural-call opening

I understand the October 26 dispute may still be pending. I'm not asking you to finish it early or to negotiate payment. I'm asking a separate question about NCS's process: who has authority to review the underlying evidence and decide that a creditor's amount is unsupported? Is that someone different from a collection representative or collection manager? What is the procedure for an unrepresented person to request that review?

Follow-on questions

  1. Does NCS's internal dispute team itself reject creditor calculations, or does it report only the client's response?
  2. If the landlord confirms its own balance, what evidence could cause NCS to reject that confirmation?
  3. Can the reviewer order correction or cessation of unsupported charges without consent from the client?
  4. If legal interpretation exceeds a collection representative's authority, who considers the legal objection?
  5. Does a represented consumer get routed to a different attorney, compliance function, or decision-maker? Can an unrepresented person request equivalent substantive review?
  6. If there is no phone transfer, what written review channel and policy governs? Who can provide the policy or a written explanation?
  7. Which specific dispute was previously closed, what was the closing date, and what documents were reviewed? How does that compare with October 26?
  8. After Reichert (2008), what procedures does NCS use to detect charges that the landlord is not legally entitled to collect? Were those procedures applied to this account?

A representative's inability to name a reviewer is evidence of difficulty accessing the process, not proof that no process exists. The call cannot force discovery. Do not publish raw recordings without checking applicable recording and privacy laws and masking sensitive data.

Records to ask of NCS

  • All written dispute intake/response dates and IDs, including the October 26 submission.
  • Each received attachment, person/role reviewing it, source materials considered and written determination.
  • The criteria for rejecting a creditor's confirmation and procedures for escalation/reconsideration.
  • Correspondence or data furnished to and received from each credit reporting agency, including dispute-notice flags and dispute verification responses.
  • Whether debt collection efforts continued while any qualifying statutory investigation was pending, and the basis claimed for doing so.

Records to ask of Firmus and the actual property creditor

  • Contract/lease provisions, charge calculations, mitigation/reletting efforts, replacement tenancy records and credits.
  • Legal entities in the creditor/owner/management relationship and the nature of the contract with NCS.
  • Vendor oversight, complaints about NCS, compliance due diligence, and any internal assessment of Reichert or other actually established violations.
  • What NCS was instructed or authorized to collect and whether the creditor corrected or rejected any amounts.

Records to ask of Experian

  • Full file disclosure under 15 USC §1681g, including available recipient information for applicable time windows.
  • For completed reinvestigations, the procedure description available under §1681i(a)(7).
  • Dated results, tradeline reports and available account-level historical score observations; historical score archives are not necessarily statutory file-disclosure items.
