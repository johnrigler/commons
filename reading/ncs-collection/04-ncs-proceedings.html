NCS Prior Litigation, Judgments and Investigations

The legal significance of an appellate ruling, a settlement, and a regulatory inquiry is different. None by itself proves what occurred in a later dispute.

Reichert v. National Credit Systems, Inc.

Published adverse federal appellate decision: Reichert v. National Credit Systems, Inc., 531 F.3d 1002 (9th Cir. July 7, 2008), docket 06-15503.

The consumer rented an apartment. The landlord referred a lease claim to NCS, which attempted to collect a $225 charge for an attorney's demand letter that was not authorized under the circumstances by the lease or law. The district court granted summary judgment on an FDCPA §1692f(1) violation. On appeal NCS relied in part on the landlord's prior accuracy and asserted its general error-prevention procedures. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, holding that those generalized assertions failed to establish NCS's statutory bona fide-error defense.

What it supports: a judicially established historical NCS violation in an apartment-debt collection case; specific reasoning about the inadequacy of relying on the client's prior accuracy as proof of reasonable error-prevention procedures.

What it does not support: a claim that NCS's 2026 procedures are identical, that a Texas landlord violated the mitigation statute, or that any unrelated company had actual knowledge of recurring Texas Chapter 392 violations. It is Ninth Circuit authority, persuasive but not binding on a Texas federal district court.

Consumer Financial Protection Bureau proceedings

The CFPB served a civil investigative demand on NCS dated October 18, 2022; NCS petitioned to set it aside. The Bureau denied that petition. A separate federal proceeding sought enforcement of the demand. The CFPB maintains a public page with the enforcement petition, amended petition, report and recommendation, and order.

What it supports: official investigatory scrutiny and procedural action concerning NCS. What it does not establish: a CFPB merits finding that NCS committed a particular consumer-law violation, a fine, or an adjudicated pattern of prohibited Texas collection practices. Avoid converting “regulatory inquiry” into “regulatory liability.”

Example of a documented settlement

Sears Jr. v. National Credit Systems, Inc., No. 3:25-cv-01078, Southern District of California: a public August 18, 2025 docket entry reports that the parties reached an agreement to settle an FCRA case; material terms were placed in a sealed recording. This is evidence that a settlement was reached, not an admission of wrongdoing or a published finding of liability. The amount is not established by the public entry reviewed.

What about other reported NCS settlements?

Other lawsuit names and purported amounts have appeared in preliminary searches and conversation. They are intentionally excluded from the confirmed tally here until the specific docket entries, order text, party identities, and outcomes are retrieved and checked. A reported settlement is not a judicial determination. Dismissal with prejudice does not automatically mean a court found the collector violated the statute.

Research test: has the 2008 problem recurred?

  1. Identify NCS's documented review procedures following Reichert, including how employees detect unauthorized lease charges.
  2. Compare documents actually received and examined in a later disputed rental account.
  3. Determine whether client confirmation was merely one input or the entire decision criterion.
  4. Compare independently verified cases without treating one case, duplicated complaints, or sealed settlement terms as independent proof of repeated misconduct.
