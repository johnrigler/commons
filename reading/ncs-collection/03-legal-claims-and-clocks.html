Potential Claims, Different Duties, Different Clocks

The original ledger charge, a later demand for payment, a failure to investigate, and a credit-reporting response are different acts. Each law has its own elements and deadlines.

Issue-spotting, not a legal opinion or assertion that any defendant violated these laws. Identify the legal creditor/owner, property manager, collector and credit furnisher separately. Actual statutory text and case-specific evidence control.

Statutory clocks

FDCPA: 15 USC §1692k(d) ordinarily requires suit within one year of the violation, not one year from the first invoice or first dispute. Distinct later actions can carry distinct dates; mere persistence of an old account is not automatically a new violation every day. Official text.

Texas §392.202: the specific 30-day written-response framework is triggered by receipt of a qualifying written notice of inaccuracy. Identify each notice and its receipt date. The available response “insufficient time” has consequences under the statute; it is not unconditional authority to ignore the dispute. Official text.

FCRA: a privately actionable furnisher-reinvestigation claim under §1681s-2(b) depends generally on notice from a reporting agency to the furnisher; the limitations rule is generally the earlier of two years after discovery of the violation or five years after its occurrence under 15 USC §1681p.

Underlying rent: landlord claims and defenses involve different Texas contract/mitigation rules. Do not copy the FDCPA one-year limit onto the October 2025 ledger posting.

Dates presently worth preserving

October 31, 2025 (charge assessed); the May 2026 written disputes and deliveries; May 20, 2026 (NCS letter); October 26, 2026 (latest dispute intake as described on call); November 4, 2026 (recorded discussion); each Experian bureau notice/reinvestigation and each actual NCS communication. None is automatically an adjudicated violation date.
