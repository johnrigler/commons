Research and public records

Empirical findings, legal rules, and institutional records support different parts of the argument. Dates and jurisdictions travel with each finding.

  1. Herd, Hoynes, Michener, and Moynihan, “Administrative Burden as a Mechanism of Inequality in Policy Implementation,” RSF 9(5), 2023. Framework of learning, compliance, and psychological costs in public services. Its application to these dispute processes is editorial analysis.
  2. Cass R. Sunstein, “Sludge Audits,” 2020; author bibliography and abstract. Auditing excessive procedural friction and its costs.
  3. The Structure and Practices of the Debt Buying Industry, FTC, January 2013. Executive summary; printed pages 34–36 and Table 9. The 12% documentation figure concerns the subset of accounts bought in March–August 2009, not current litigation success or the share of debts that were invalid.
  4. How Debt Collectors Are Transforming the Business of State Courts, Pew Charitable Trusts, 6 May 2020. Research review, methods, representation, and default findings. Jurisdictions and study periods vary; associations do not isolate the causal effect of representation.
  5. Texas Rules of Evidence, effective 2 July 2026. Rules 801(e)(2)(A), 803(6), and 902(10). Foundation and party-statement rules, with their conditions; relevance and other admissibility requirements remain separate.
  6. Texas Rules of Civil Procedure, official compilation checked 22 October 2026. Rules 93(8), 198.2–198.3, 500.1(e), 503.5, and 508.3. Official index lists the compilation as last amended 1 October 2026. Forum, issue, and case date determine applicability.
  7. Lake Jackson Medical Spa, Ltd. v. Gaytan, No. 20-0802, Supreme Court of Texas, 25 February 2022. Pages 14–15 discuss judicial admissions in pleadings; this is an unrelated medical case used for that legal distinction.
  8. Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code, Chapter 154. Sections 154.021–.023 and .073: referral, mediation, and confidentiality. Court-ordered attendance and voluntary agreement are distinct.
  9. Fair Debt Collection Practices Act: CFPB Annual Report 2024, September 2024. Sections 1.2.1 and 1.2.4 discuss rental debts not owed and verification problems. Reported consumer complaints do not adjudicate every underlying dispute.
  10. Texas Property Code §91.006. Landlord's duty to mitigate after abandonment in breach of a lease; purported waiver is void.
  11. Texas State Law Library, “Ending the Lease”. Official explanation of replacement-tenancy efforts and rent liability after early departure.
  12. 15 U.S.C. §1681i. Reinvestigation, relevant information, correction or deletion, and descriptions of the procedure. Official text through Public Law 119-120.
  13. 15 U.S.C. §1681e(b). Reporting agencies' accuracy procedures.
  14. 15 U.S.C. §1681s–2(b). Furnisher duties after notice from a reporting agency, including review and correction notices to other nationwide agencies.
  15. FTC, “Disputing Errors on Your Credit Reports”. Commercial reporting, consequences, and disputes with each company carrying an error.
  16. e-OSCAR, “Getting Started”. The system operator's account of routing disputes, furnisher responses, and corrections. Describes the route, not the adequacy of any particular investigation.
  17. Annual Report of Credit and Consumer Reporting Complaints, CFPB, December 2025. Sections 2–3: January 2024–June 2025 complaints, prior-dispute disagreements, repeated response language, and company-reported outcomes. Complaint volumes are not population error rates.
  18. Grapevine Municipal Court, “Online Payments”. Conviction warning, alternative routes, online deferral payment requirement, and exclusions.
  19. Austin Municipal Court, “Ways to Request a Deferral for a Moving Violation”. Published course and no-course choices and the $50 fee difference; not a lawyer's fee.
  20. California DMV, “Laws and Rules of the Road,” Traffic Violator School. Eligible citation's driving-record and insurer-reporting treatment.
  21. North Carolina G.S. 20-141(o). Lesser improper-equipment offense, eligibility limits, license points, and insurance surcharge.
  22. North Carolina Judicial Branch, “Traffic Violations”. Representation, negotiation, self-representation, and county-specific court procedures.
  23. Targeted Fines and Fees Against Communities of Color, U.S. Commission on Civil Rights, September 2017. Printed page 72, findings on revenue dependence and traffic-enforcement incentives.
  24. Investigation of the Ferguson Police Department, DOJ Civil Rights Division, 4 March 2015. Summary and section III: revenue expectations and their connection to police and court practices.
  25. Town of Henderson, Louisiana, audited financial statements, year ended 30 June 2025. General Fund Budgetary Comparison Schedule, printed page 37 / PDF page 40, actual column. The 78.0% share is calculated from that schedule.

The arguments and proposed presentation standards are editorial analysis. Research samples, complaint data, legal requirements, and findings about specific institutions are identified where used. Published national and local findings support comparison across institutions without assuming that every dispute has the same facts or outcome.
