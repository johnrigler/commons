Companies in the process

An argument explains a recurring problem. A company profile identifies an organization and its role. A case supplies the evidence connecting the two.

These profiles cover businesses that assemble credit information, collect accounts, purchase debts, or represent creditors. Each profile names its scope, identifies selected public records, and connects to reusable arguments. Different legal entities remain separately identifiable even when they share a brand.

Company descriptions establish what a business says it does. A regulator's complaint states allegations. An investigative order addresses an investigation. A settlement or consent order has the scope and conditions stated in that record. Those distinctions matter when reusing a reference.

The Companies view provides a separate reference download for each profile. A final document can cite a company profile for organizational background and an article for the relevant reasoning. The underlying case records establish the actor, account, dates, conduct, and requested action.

Company profile · Credit reporting

Experian

A credit reporting company receives information from furnishers, distributes reports, and processes disputes about those reports.

John Rigler · Sources checked 22 October 2026 · Unsigned, AI-assisted research draft

Identity and business role

This profile concerns Experian Information Solutions, Inc., the U.S. consumer reporting company named in the CFPB's 2025 lawsuit. The CFPB describes its business as organizing consumer data into reports sold to creditors and other businesses making eligibility decisions.[1] The name “Experian” alone should not substitute for the entity identified on a particular report or notice.

Selected official record: a pending lawsuit

The CFPB sued on 7 January 2025. Its second amended complaint alleges inadequate dispute reinvestigations, failure to remove inaccurate or unverified information, improper reinsertion, and excessive reliance on furnishers. These are allegations. The CFPB's case page reports that the court denied a motion to dismiss on 22 October 2025 and that discovery is ongoing, following a January 2026 ruling on affirmative defenses. The listed record does not report a final merits judgment.[1]

The consumer-facing process

Experian's official dispute page provides online and mail routes.[2] The CFPB explains that a reporting dispute and a dispute with the business supplying the information address separate responsibilities.[5]

Why this company belongs in the argument

The credit-reporting article examines the pressure created when a disputed balance circulates as an eligibility signal. Here the organizational question is who maintained the report, what information reached the reinvestigation, and what response followed. A profile supplies background; the report, dispute, supporting documents, and result establish the sequence in a particular case.

Company profile · Credit reporting

Equifax

A reporting business can be both the distributor of disputed information and the institution a consumer must ask to correct it.

John Rigler · Sources checked 22 October 2026 · Unsigned, AI-assisted research draft

Identity and business role

The selected enforcement record names Equifax, Inc. and Equifax Information Services LLC. This profile uses the latter as its consumer-reporting entity reference while preserving both names in the record's scope. The CFPB describes Equifax as assembling consumer reports sold to businesses evaluating loans, jobs, housing, and other products.[3]

Selected official record: a 2025 consent order

On 17 January 2025, the CFPB issued a consent order recording findings concerning dispute reinvestigations, reinsertion of deleted information, notices, accuracy procedures, and identity-theft blocks. It also found excessive deference to furnishers and the sale of inaccurate scores and attributes after test code entered a production system. The order required a $15 million civil penalty and compliance measures.[3] The administrative docket lists the order and stipulation.[4] This is a dated enforcement record, whose terms should be read directly when applying it.

The consumer-facing process

The CFPB's current guide links Equifax's official dispute page and contact page. It recommends identifying the specific error and retaining copies of the dispute and supporting documents.[5]

What a company reference adds

The broader reporting argument concerns how difficult correction can coexist with powerful downstream consequences. A company reference keeps the institution identifiable while the case record supplies the exact information disputed, its source, the result, and any subsequent reporting. A regulator's earlier findings do not decide the accuracy of a later account.

Company profile · Credit and background reporting

TransUnion

A common brand can encompass different reporting products and separately named legal entities.

John Rigler · Sources checked 22 October 2026 · Unsigned, AI-assisted research draft

Identity and business role

The 2023 record discussed here names Trans Union LLC and its then-subsidiary, TransUnion Rental Screening Solutions, Inc. The CFPB describes the latter as providing tenant and employment background reports to property owners, managers, employers, and other screening businesses.[6] The profile's entity reference uses Trans Union LLC as named in that record. A tenant-screening report and a nationwide credit report should each be matched to the issuer shown on the document.

Selected official record: a 2023 settlement

The CFPB and FTC filed a joint complaint on 12 October 2023 alleging inaccurate eviction reporting and inadequate disclosure of third-party record sources. The court entered a stipulated order on 18 October 2023 requiring accuracy and disclosure measures, $11 million in consumer redress, and a $4 million penalty.[6] The order states that the defendants neither admit nor deny the allegations, apart from specified jurisdictional facts.[7] Its scope concerns the named defendants and background-screening practices.

The consumer-facing process

TransUnion's consumer support page separates disputes, credit freezes, fraud alerts, and subscription support.[8] The CFPB also lists credit-dispute routes.[5]

Follow the information to its source

The connection to traceable records is practical: an adverse report becomes harder to challenge when the source, event, or outcome cannot be reconstructed. A case should preserve the actual product, issuer, record source, disputed field, and decision. The company profile makes that organizational background reusable across different disputes.

Company profile · Rental collections

National Credit Systems

Rental balances can pass from a property owner's records into a specialist collection process.

John Rigler · Sources checked 22 October 2026 · Unsigned, AI-assisted research draft

Identity and business role

This profile identifies National Credit Systems, Inc., using NCS as its abbreviation. Its company page describes a receivables-management business focused on residential and commercial property owners, particularly apartment collections. Its listed services include pre-collection, standard collection, legal services, garnishment of existing judgments, and account-purchase options.[9] Those options make the role in a particular account important: collection for an owner and purchase of an account are different relationships.

Selected official record: an investigation procedure

On 20 December 2022, the CFPB denied NCS's petition to set aside a civil investigative demand and directed a response within 21 days of service of the order. The decision addressed NCS's challenge to the Bureau's authority to issue the demand.[11] It is an investigative procedural record. It is not a finding that a consumer owed a particular balance or an adjudication of a collection violation.

The consumer-facing process

NCS's official contact page identifies the company and its public contact routes.[10] A company name on correspondence should be matched to the actual sender and creditor named there.

The records that carry a rental claim

The rent-acceleration article examines the basis for charging future rent; the records article examines the account's traceability. In a rental dispute, useful case questions include which lease provision supports each charge, which payments or credits were applied, who supplied the ledger, and who claims the right to collect. The profile provides organizational context without supplying those missing account facts.

Company profile · Creditor representation

Rausch Sturm LLP

A law firm's name identifies the representative. The creditor, account owner, and evidence remain separate parts of a claim.

John Rigler · Sources checked 22 October 2026 · Unsigned, AI-assisted research draft

Identity and business role

Rausch Sturm LLP identifies itself as a law firm representing financial institutions, creditor issuers, and creditors. Its consumer material describes acting for a bank or creditor that has enlisted its services.[12] That description establishes the firm's stated business role. It does not establish who owns a specific account or what documents support a particular lawsuit.

The firm's published process

The official website lists written disputes by mail or email, a contact form, and a separate complaint route. It also states that payments should be payable to its client.[12] These are the firm's descriptions of its consumer-facing process; they are distinct from a court order or a regulator's finding.

Representation and proof answer different questions

The civil-process article examines how a claim can be resolved through procedure. The records article examines whether the account and claimed transfer can be traced. A reusable company profile identifies the law firm; the case record should identify the plaintiff, the firm's role, the account claimed, and the evidence offered for ownership and amount.

A letter, pleading, or signature block provides a concrete name to match. Similar-sounding names, remembered spellings, and an old directory entry are insufficient by themselves to establish that two firms are the same legal entity. This profile covers the current firm under the name used on its own website. It makes no finding about an individual suit.

Company profile · Debt purchasing

Midland Funding LLC

The account owner and the business communicating about the account can be different legal entities.

John Rigler · Sources checked 22 October 2026 · Unsigned, AI-assisted research draft

Identity and business role

Midland Funding LLC is described by its affiliate's official material as a purchaser and owner of accounts charged off by original lenders. Midland Credit Management, Inc. (MCM) services Midland Funding accounts and accounts MCM itself owns. The site identifies both within the Encore Capital Group family.[13] These relationships explain why a communication may name more than one business.

Selected official record: a 2020 judgment

The CFPB's 2020 action named Encore Capital Group, Midland Funding, Midland Credit Management, and Asset Acceptance Capital Corp. It alleged violations of an earlier order and federal consumer law. On 16 October 2020, the court entered a stipulated final judgment requiring $79,308.81 in consumer redress and a $15 million civil penalty, with disclosure and other conduct provisions.[15] Separately, the administrative docket records termination of the 2015 consent order on 20 October 2020.[16] These are distinct procedural events; the 2015 order should not be described as still in force solely because it appears in historical background.

Connect the buyer to the account

The traceable-account article asks how a claimed transfer identifies the specific account. Naming a debt buyer supplies an entity reference. The sale records, account identifiers, balance history, and other case evidence must still establish the particular claim.

MCM's Midland Funding page provides its public explanation and contact route.[13] A company statement that a reported name indicates ownership is not a substitute for examining the actual disputed record.

Company profile · Debt purchasing, servicing, and reporting

Midland Credit Management, Inc.

A servicer may communicate, collect, and furnish information while the account is owned by that company or an affiliate.

John Rigler · Sources checked 22 October 2026 · Unsigned, AI-assisted research draft

Identity and business role

Midland Credit Management, Inc. (MCM) describes itself as both a debt purchaser and servicer. Its FAQ distinguishes MCM-owned accounts from those it services for affiliates including Midland Funding LLC.[14] The two names should remain separate in a case's list of organizations and roles.

A published reporting policy

MCM's FAQ describes a policy of delaying credit reporting for the first six months following its initial notification, with conditions under which reporting will not begin, and requesting deletion after an account is paid or settled. It also lists a Consumer Resolution Center for account issues.[14] These are company policy statements checked on the date of this profile. They do not guarantee a credit score outcome, establish a disputed debt, or describe every reporting business's policy.

Selected official record: a shared 2020 action

MCM was among the named companies in the CFPB's 2020 Encore action. The court entered a stipulated final judgment on 16 October 2020, including a $15 million civil penalty, consumer redress, and disclosure and conduct requirements.[15] The separate 2015 administrative order was terminated on 20 October 2020.[16] These dated records do not decide a new individual account dispute.

Separate ownership, servicing, and reporting

The credit-reporting article concerns the consequences of distributing a balance; the records article concerns the basis for the claim. A case can connect both arguments to this company profile while preserving who owned the account, who sent each communication, who furnished the disputed entry, and what each entity did after receiving a dispute.
