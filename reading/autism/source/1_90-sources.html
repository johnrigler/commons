Autism: assessment and benefit standards

Clinical assessment, service eligibility, and disability-benefit decisions have different purposes.

  1. National Institute of Mental Health, Autism Spectrum Disorder. Differences in support needs and assessment across ages, including adult referral and developmental history.
  2. CDC, Clinical Screening for Autism Spectrum Disorder. Reports AAP recommendations for autism-specific screening at 18 and 24 months. Screening and diagnostic evaluation remain separate steps.
  3. CDC, Clinical Testing and Diagnosis for Autism Spectrum Disorder. Developmental history, professional observation, diagnostic criteria, and the limits of a single assessment tool.
  4. CDC, Accessing Services for Autism Spectrum Disorder. Early-intervention and school evaluation routes; a medical diagnosis is not a prerequisite for requesting those evaluations. Services have their own eligibility criteria.
  5. CDC, Autism Spectrum Disorder FAQ, adult diagnosis section. Adult assessment and the challenges of reconstructing early development; page dated 1 October 2026.
  6. SSA, Adult Mental Disorders, listing 12.10 and sections 12.00C–F. Autism medical and functional criteria, evidence over time, and the effects of structured or supportive settings.
  7. SSA, Childhood Mental Disorders, listing 112.10. Autism listing for ages three to under eighteen. Section 112.00A identifies separate developmental-disorder criteria for children under three.
  8. SSA, SSI for Children. Childhood disability, duration, financial eligibility, parental deeming, and possible Medicaid routes apart from SSI.
  9. SSA, SSI Eligibility, 2026 edition. Adult and childhood disability definitions, income and resources, and other eligibility conditions.
  10. SSA, How Does Someone Become Eligible?. SSDI work and medical requirements, and the adult-child route for qualifying disability beginning before age 22. Benefit onset and diagnosis dates should not be conflated.
  11. SSA, Evidentiary Requirements. Objective medical evidence establishes a medically determinable impairment; medical and nonmedical evidence inform severity and function.
  12. SSA Publication 05-10029, Disability Benefits. Sequential evaluation: when a severe impairment does not meet or medically equal a listing, evaluation proceeds to work-related steps.
  13. 20 CFR §416.926a, Functional equivalence for children. Six functional domains, age-peer comparison, supportive settings, and the severity and duration requirements for this route.
  14. SSA, Medical Evidence. Do not delay filing for records; agency evidence requests and possible examinations. This does not dispense with the evidence needed for a decision.

The discussion of assessment as an access barrier and the proposed organization of evidence are editorial analysis. This entry explains selected U.S. federal benefit rules and links the separate New Mexico/Texas series. It does not diagnose a person, estimate how many adults are undiagnosed, or treat a screening score or autism diagnosis as an automatic benefit award. No independent clinical or legal review is recorded.
