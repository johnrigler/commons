Calling the collector: a recollection and a performance

The person who initiates the call can also document the process, including the point at which a scripted, stressful exchange becomes visible.

I am describing a way to deal with debt collectors: call them rather than wait for them to call you. Initiate the conversation, state why you called, try to reach a person who can give a substantive answer, and keep the complete record. The record can include the receptionist, a transfer, the scripted preamble and the repeated effort to reach someone able to respond.

A collector may call me a customer. I do not see an ordinary customer-service relationship, or a service-level agreement obligating the company to resolve my concern. In my view, the relationship is adversarial by design. Collection scripts can add pressure to people already in financial difficulty. I also question what this process does to the people required to perform it. My comparison with prison guards or law enforcement is an analogy about occupational stress, not a clinical assertion.

My proposal is to capture an interaction lawfully, preserve the original, mask sensitive speech with a beep in a publishable derivative, and make each derivative a separately identifiable artifact. A compilation can cover several calls, including what I was actually trying to find out.

What becomes an artifact

An original recording should remain unchanged and private by default. Its redacted public version needs its own hash and CID. A compilation of several calls must name its segments and receive a separate identity. A text transcript or a commentary edition is another artifact, not a substitute for the sound.

Any Litecoin signature must cover the exact edition being attested to. The address printed above identifies the intended signer but does not prove control of the key. Before recording or sharing an actual telephone call, consent and privacy requirements must be checked for all applicable jurisdictions, and sensitive information must be removed from both audio and text.
