# Calling the collector: a recollection and a performance

*Recollection, author commentary and a deliberately performed escalation of tone. Recorded in writing on 25 October 2026.*

I am describing a way to deal with debt collectors: call them rather than wait for them to call you. Initiate the conversation, state the reason for calling, seek a person who can give a substantive answer, and keep a record of the whole process. That process may include a receptionist, a transfer, an agent reading a preamble and repeated attempts to reach someone with responsibility for the disputed issue.

A collector may regard the person as a customer, but I do not regard this as an ordinary customer-service relationship. There is no service-level agreement under which the company undertakes to resolve my concern as my service provider. I experience the relationship as adversarial by design. A scripted collection preamble can increase pressure on someone already having financial difficulty. That stress can be traumatic. I also wonder what happens to the people who deliver these scripts, and whether repeated participation in an adversarial process imposes a psychological cost on them. I compare that possibility, as an analogy rather than a clinical finding, with occupational stress among prison guards and law-enforcement personnel.

My proposal is to make the interaction visible. Record the full chain of calls where lawful, distinguish what I was asking from what each person actually answered, and be able to publish a redacted recording without disclosing account numbers, identity-verification answers or other sensitive information. The raw recording, the edited recording and any compiled recording should be distinct, verifiable digital artifacts.

## The performed escalation

I then said I would change tone and engage in character play, in the same sense that someone reading a collection script performs a role. The following passage is a **performance**, not a transcript of an actual call or a statement of intent to confront anyone in person. Profanity, repetition and the deliberately provocative Atlanta line are retained to show the change in intensity.

> You know, none of this would be necessary if somebody would read their fucking email, or maybe, maybe, maybe, just maybe respond to the registered letter that I sent to you that I have referenced over and over and over again, and I have etched its fucking signature onto the blockchain 4,700 times. Would somebody please respond to me? Because this is, this, you have no legal basis for this. This would fall apart immediately in court.
>
> Now I can go call and try to get a lawyer, or I can head out to Atlanta and, you know, find the titty bar closest to your law firm so that I can speak to you in person, right? Am I right? Ha ha ha, funny, funny, funny.

I then asked:

> Now do you see how in that moment, do you see how in that moment stress increased?

**What I intended to demonstrate:** the words and their delivery can change the emotional temperature of a conversation even when the underlying demand is unchanged. A collection script can do this in one direction. A consumer can deliberately make the effect audible in the other direction, then identify the effect rather than let it silently control the exchange.

The reference to “4,700 times” is part of the expressive performance; this record does **not** assert a verified count of Litecoin transactions. Likewise, the claim that a debt has “no legal basis” is the speaker’s position within the performance, not a legal finding. No raw audio was provided with this written record, so vocal intensity, timing and volume are not independently preserved here.

## Recording and publication model

The proposed Commons recording tool should preserve the original captured audio privately, never alter it, and calculate its own content hash. A publishable derivative should replace selected spans with audible beeps, retain a private edit log, and receive a different hash and CID. A compilation may contain several phone calls, a receptionist, transfers and subsequent attempts, with source links and a segment timeline. Each compilation is a new artifact, not the original call.

A signature by the author's Litecoin identity should bind **one exact edition** of this recollection or a recording manifest. Displaying a Litecoin address is not itself a signature. Nothing on this page establishes a completed wallet signature, an IPFS pin, or a Litecoin transaction.

Recording and publishing real calls require checking the applicable consent, privacy and disclosure rules before capture or release. A beeped copy protects only the portions actually masked; call metadata and surrounding speech can still reveal sensitive information.
