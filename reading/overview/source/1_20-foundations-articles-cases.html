Commons / Foundations and connections

Subjects meet in articles

Foundations organize broad subjects. Articles develop issues. Cases connect those issues to particular events and evidence.

A foundation is a subject or organizing concern, such as Real Estate, Religion or procedural burden. An article is a reusable document developing a particular issue. It can connect more than one foundation. A case can refer to several articles.

An example at the intersection

Real Estate and Religion can be separate foundations. A proposed Parsonage Exemption article would connect both. Its placement follows the subject; it does not have to derive from the administrative-burden article. Its substantive scope and sources still need to be developed.

The same article can serve different cases

The burden article might help someone describe a credit dispute, a traffic-ticket process or a VA matter. It supplies a way to examine the process. The individual case supplies the evidence showing whether that description fits.

Other articles may have no useful connection to administrative burden. Commons does not require a single argument to explain every subject. These relationships organize a view of the material; they are not admission rules for the stream.

Keep the version identifiable

A case should identify the article edition it uses. A later improvement can become a new edition while the earlier version remains available for understanding what was actually sent or relied on.
