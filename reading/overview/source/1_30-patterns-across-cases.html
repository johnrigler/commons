Recognizing a shared pattern

Different institutions can present similar problems of records, explanations, effort and consequences.

John can move from a credit dispute to a speeding ticket or a veterans' benefits question because he recognizes a process structure. Someone receives a demand or decision, seeks its basis, tries to correct or challenge it, and bears the consequences while doing so.

This is a way to generate useful questions. It does not establish that the same law, deadline, evidence or remedy applies to each situation.

Make the burden visible

Record what was requested, which records were supplied, what had to be repeated, how long each step took, and which questions remained unanswered. Distinguish steps that serve a necessary purpose from obstacles whose cost or complexity is excessive. Sludge describes the latter concern.

Let the case test the comparison

The comparison becomes useful when it helps identify something specific to investigate. A label alone does not show why a charge is unsupported or a process is unreasonable. Exact documents and a chronology let other people examine the connection.

John is not a veteran; no personal VA claim is presented here. The VA and traffic examples are areas for contribution and investigation. A particular case needs its own knowledgeable participants and governing sources.
