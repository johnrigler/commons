Sources and scope

  1. Texas Rules of Evidence, effective 2 July 2026. Rule 801(e)(2)(A), PDF pages 46–47; Rule 408, PDF pages 14–15. Supports the distinction between an opposing party's statement and qualifying compromise negotiations.
  2. Lake Jackson Medical Spa, Ltd. v. Gaytan, No. 20-0802, Supreme Court of Texas, 25 February 2022. Pages 14–15 discuss judicial admissions in live and superseded pleadings. An unrelated medical case used only for that distinction.
  3. Texas Rules of Civil Procedure, current compilation linked by the Texas Judicial Branch on 22 October 2026. Rules 93(8), 198.2–198.3, 500.1(e), 508.2(a)(4), and 508.3(b)(3); PDF pages 72, 164, 241, and 269–270. The official index lists the rules as last amended 1 October 2026. Current numbering differs from some earlier compilations.
  4. Repairing a Broken System: Protecting Consumers in Debt Collection Litigation and Arbitration, Federal Trade Commission, July 2010. Printed pages 16–17, PDF pages 27–28, discuss unfamiliar claimants and information needed in complaints. Historical national research, not a measure of the specific admission pattern or current Texas courtroom practice.
