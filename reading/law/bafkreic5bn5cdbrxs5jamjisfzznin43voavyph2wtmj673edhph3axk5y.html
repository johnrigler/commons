Unsupported rent acceleration · Duties and time

Which deadline belongs to which party?

Keep the underlying rental charge, the investigation of a dispute, and the deadline for bringing a claim connected to their own legal requirements.

LcudkPQzLuuqsnzHSmJ7iLREaHStvPKRVb · 24 October 2026 · Unsigned AI-assisted reference; no independent legal review

The underlying objection remains unsupported rent acceleration

The Commons uses unsupported rent acceleration for a demand for future rent whose lease basis, calculation, mitigation, reletting and credits have not been established by the material supplied. Collection and reporting laws add duties around that same objection. They do not replace it with a generic fraud allegation or a payment negotiation.

Keep the entities distinct

Role in a rental disputeQuestion to examine
Property owner or managerWho created and authorized the rental balance? What lease and historical accounting support it? Identify the legally responsible owner, lessor and manager separately.
Collector and reported furnisherWhat was collected, investigated and supplied to the bureau? Which written notices reached this entity?
Reporting agencyWhat dispute and attachments did the bureau receive, forward and reinvestigate? What results did it provide?

A property manager being in Texas does not transfer a third-party-collector-specific duty to it. A collector being outside Texas does not, by itself, determine whether Texas law applies to collection of a Texas rental account. Coverage and forum require their own analysis.

What certified delivery establishes

A delivery record helps establish when a particular recipient received a notice. The relevant law determines whether that notice triggered a duty, what had to happen and when. Keep the exact packet, delivery evidence, recipient, applicable provision, response and later conduct together. Sending to one entity does not automatically establish receipt by every other entity.

A courtesy email or portal follow-up should remain connected to the original notice. It does not retroactively make an earlier response timely. Repeated uploads do not automatically create daily damages or restart a lawsuit's limitation period.

Preserve the complaint about delay

A sender can maintain that a substantive answer is long overdue. The dispute can include what each organization did after receiving a request as well as whether it can supply supporting material later.

Record that position and the elapsed time in the completed private correspondence. A legal assertion that a particular deadline expired must name the provision, recipient, triggering receipt, required response and actual failure. It is a further assertion to say that expiry permanently bars the underlying charge or excludes all later evidence. The cited mitigation provision establishes no such general cutoff, and §392.202(e) expressly contemplates a later accuracy determination.

Records made later may describe earlier events. They cannot cause historical reletting efforts to have occurred. Identify when records were created, what was actually done during the vacancy, and what evidence was available when the balance was demanded or verified. A later explanation does not by itself resolve a claim about earlier actionable collection or reporting conduct.

A timely denial can satisfy a particular statement requirement while leaving the adequacy of an investigation in dispute. A request for every source document, substantive lease proof, regulatory verification and court discovery are distinct requirements. Preserve the precise failure being alleged.

Texas housing

Texas Property Code §91.006 — Mitigation after abandonment

Who it applies to
Landlord of the leased premises; identify the legally responsible owner/lessor.
What starts the duty
Abandonment in breach of a lease.
Time limit
Duty concerns reasonable efforts during the relevant vacancy period; this section specifies no 30-day response deadline for a records request.
Required action
Take reasonable steps to reduce avoidable rental loss; a purported waiver of the duty is void.
Consequence or remedy
Failure to mitigate can reduce recoverable loss to the extent it reasonably could have been avoided.
Scope and limits
A missing answer does not itself prove no mitigation occurred. The lease, events, efforts and avoidable amount require evidence.

Texas Property Code §§92.103–92.109 — Security deposits and a distinct 30-day rule

Who it applies to
Residential landlord as defined by §92.001(2); a management agent is not automatically that landlord.
What starts the duty
Surrender of possession and the written forwarding-address condition in §92.107.
Time limit
Generally refund within 30 days after surrender; the refund/itemization obligation is conditioned on the written forwarding address.
Required action
Refund the deposit or account for lawful deductions, subject to statutory exceptions.
Consequence or remedy
§92.109 provides bad-faith presumptions, deposit remedies and specified forfeitures; §92.109(b) addresses suit for damages to the premises.
Scope and limits
This is not a blanket 30-day deadline to prove accelerated rent or an automatic forfeiture of every lease claim.

Texas Finance Code §392.202(a)–(e) — Written disputes and the 30-day statement

Who it applies to
Covered third-party debt collectors; determine the actual collector separately from the source creditor or property manager.
What starts the duty
Receipt by that collector of qualifying written notice identifying an inaccuracy in its file.
Time limit
Written statement no later than day 30 after receipt. Admitted inaccuracies: correction within five business days.
Required action
Investigate and send a statement admitting or denying the inaccuracy, or explaining insufficient investigation time. Collection must cease as provided in (a). The insufficient-time route requires interim file changes, recipient notices and cessation under (d).
Consequence or remedy
On completion notify the consumer. Under (e), a determination of accuracy permits reporting and collection to resume. Civil remedies are addressed by §392.403.
Scope and limits
A denial is not an obligation to send every requested document. Missing the response deadline does not automatically erase the underlying lease debt; a later investigation does not make an earlier response timely.

15 U.S.C. §1692g; 12 CFR §1006.38 — Validation period and collection pause

Who it applies to
Covered debt collector receiving a timely written dispute.
What starts the duty
Written dispute submitted within the validation period, generally 30 days after validation information is received.
Time limit
30 days is principally the consumer’s dispute period. This provision sets no fixed number of days for the collector to provide verification.
Required action
Stop collecting the disputed portion pending the required verification response; Regulation F recognizes a portal that accepts consumer communications.
Consequence or remedy
Collection can resume after compliant verification; stopping indefinitely is different from responding within a fixed deadline.
Scope and limits
Do not substitute this rule for Texas §392.202. Verification is not necessarily disclosure of every document needed to litigate the merits.

12 CFR §1006.38 and official interpretations — Portal submissions and duplicative disputes

Who it applies to
Covered debt collectors and timely written consumer disputes.
What starts the duty
Use of a medium through which the collector accepts consumer communications.
Time limit
Validation-period conditions apply; no daily-upload entitlement or automatic new deadline.
Required action

Scope and limits

Federal reporting

15 U.S.C. §1681i(a) — Bureau reinvestigation and results

Who it applies to
Consumer reporting agencies.
What starts the duty
Receipt of a qualifying dispute by the bureau.
Time limit
Generally 30 days; up to 15 additional days for qualifying relevant information during that period. Results generally within five business days after completion; procedure description within 15 days after request under (7).
Required action
Reinvestigate reasonably, forward relevant information, consider relevant consumer material, and correct/delete/modify as the statute requires.
Consequence or remedy
Inaccurate, incomplete or unverifiable information requires appropriate correction or deletion; qualifying violations may support civil claims.
Scope and limits
The bureau’s clock is not automatically triggered by a letter delivered only to the source creditor or property manager. Deletion from a report does not itself extinguish a contractual debt.

15 U.S.C. §1681s–2(a)–(d) — Furnisher duties and private-claim boundaries

Who it applies to
The entity furnishing information to a consumer reporting agency; do not automatically assign that role to the property manager.
What starts the duty
For (b), notice from a bureau under §1681i(a)(2); direct disputes and known inaccuracies implicate different provisions.
Time limit
The (b) investigation must finish within the applicable bureau investigation period.
Required action
Investigate, review relevant bureau-supplied material, report results and make required corrections. (a)(3) addresses disputed-status reporting.
Consequence or remedy
Privately enforceable investigation claims generally arise under (b); (c)–(d) restrict private enforcement of (a), including its regulations.
Scope and limits
Sending documents directly to the collector or source creditor does not by itself establish the bureau-notice trigger for a (b) damages claim.

12 CFR §1022.43 — Direct disputes to furnishers

Who it applies to
The actual furnisher receiving a qualifying direct dispute at a specified or otherwise qualifying address.
What starts the duty
Receipt of an adequately identified account, disputed information, explanation and supporting material, subject to exceptions.
Time limit
Generally the applicable §1681i investigation period; frivolous/irrelevant determination notice within five business days of determination.
Required action

Consequence or remedy
A regulatory duty exists; private damages enforcement must be analyzed with §1681s–2(c)–(d).
Scope and limits
No automatic email receipt is specified here. A new upload is not automatically a fresh independent investigation duty.

Texas Finance Code §§392.403–392.404 — Texas collection remedies

Who it applies to
Persons with an actionable Chapter 392 violation and the required injury or basis for relief.

Texas Business & Commerce Code §§17.45, 17.50 — Deceptive-practices remedies

Who it applies to
Qualifying consumers and actionable practices; consider statutory definitions, exclusions and any tie-in claim.
What starts the duty
A covered practice causing the required harm, with additional findings for enhanced damages.
Time limit
Remedy provision; see §17.505 for notice.
Required action
Establish the applicable claim, causation and damages. Knowing or intentional conduct can support enhanced damages under the specified rules.
Consequence or remedy
Economic damages, qualifying mental-anguish damages, fees and potentially up to treble damages, depending on findings and claim.

Texas Business & Commerce Code §17.505 — DTPA pre-suit notice

Who it applies to
Consumer contemplating a covered damages suit and the intended defendant.
What starts the duty
Notice reasonably detailing the complaint, damages and reasonably incurred expenses, including attorney fees if any.
Time limit
Generally at least 60 days before filing; statutory exceptions apply.
Required action
Give the required written notice and particulars.
Consequence or remedy
Noncompliance can lead to abatement while notice is supplied and the period runs.
Scope and limits
This is a claimant’s pre-suit requirement, not 60 days for a creditor to prove the debt. An ordinary follow-up email is not automatically a compliant notice.

15 U.S.C. §1692k — FDCPA damages, fees and filing period

Who it applies to
Each plaintiff with a viable FDCPA claim against a covered collector.
What starts the duty
A proven violation of that plaintiff’s rights; separate proof is needed for each person.
Time limit
Generally file within one year of the violation under (d); tolling issues require specific analysis.
Required action
Prove the claim and any actual damages caused. Additional individual statutory damages are discretionary up to $1,000 per action; successful actions can recover reasonable fees/costs.
Consequence or remedy
Two qualifying plaintiffs can each receive an award. Multiple calls do not each generate $1,000.
Scope and limits
Do not automatically multiply awards by defendants acting together for the same injury. §1692k has no separate punitive-damages award; other claims may have other remedies.

Judicial applications

Austin Hill Country Realty v. Palisades Plaza, 948 S.W.2d 293 (Tex. 1997); subsequent applications

Reasonable reletting efforts; failure to mitigate as an affirmative defense and proof of avoidable loss. The linked 2026 opinion is a commercial-lease application; it is not a ruling in the author’s case.

Lucero v. Debt Recovery Attorneys, D.N.M., No. 1:19-cv-00106, Doc. 97 (2020)

Rejected another statutory recovery after satisfaction of the same indivisible FDCPA injury. A district-court decision, not a universal ruling for independent violations.

Horton Brothers damages opinion, D. Del., No. 02-1656 (2007)

Awarded a separate $1,000 to each qualifying plaintiff. Shared account status does not automatically establish each person’s claim.

Kearney v. Salomon Smith Barney, 39 Cal.4th 95 (2006)

Illustrates interstate recording choice-of-law analysis; not a ruling that California governs a New Mexico–Georgia call.

Damages follow the claim

The federal collector's $1,000 additional-damages provision does not decide every defendant's liability or cap an entire rental, collection and reporting case.

Claim or provisionPotential relief and required distinction
FDCPA §1692kActual damages caused by violations; discretionary additional individual damages up to $1,000 per action; reasonable fees/costs in a successful action. No separate punitive award under this provision.
FCRA §§1681n–1681oFor qualifying willful noncompliance, actual damages or $100–$1,000 statutory damages, possible punitive damages, and fees/costs. Negligent noncompliance requires actual damages. Establish a privately enforceable duty.
Texas Finance Code §§392.403–392.404Applicable actual damages, injunctions and successful-action fees/costs; a minimum $100 for specified violations under (e), including §392.202. DTPA-linked remedies require their own analysis.
Texas DTPA §17.50Qualifying damages and fees, with knowing/intentional findings potentially supporting enhanced damages. An ordinary complaint is not automatically a completed DTPA claim or notice.
Texas security-deposit provisionsSpecific bad-faith remedies and forfeitures concern the deposit and conditions in the statute. They do not establish automatic trebling of the whole collection balance.

Two people and two companies

Each person must establish their own viable claim. Separate qualifying FDCPA plaintiffs can each receive an additional award up to $1,000; the maximum is discretionary. Being two lessees does not automatically establish two violations or two compensable injuries. The multiple-plaintiff opinion illustrates separate awards.

Two companies' involvement does not automatically double an award for the same injury. Lucero, a New Mexico federal district decision, rejected a further statutory recovery after that indivisible claim was satisfied. Independent violations, other claims and the controlling forum can require different analysis. Joint and several responsibility concerns who can satisfy an award, not permission to collect the same award twice.

Examine the source party's own conduct

A property manager or creditor's exclusion from the federal FDCPA does not establish immunity under Texas collection law, the lease, applicable landlord provisions or other actionable conduct. Determine which entity is legally responsible for each act. A coordinated scheme need not be proved to examine a company's own actionable conduct; allegations of collusion require supporting facts and a viable legal basis.

Evidence and testimony have a purpose

Connect each proposed witness and record to the calculation, mitigation, instructions, investigation, reporting or loss at issue. An organizational deposition under Federal Rule 30(b)(6) can reach the organization's reasonably available knowledge. Remote depositions and remote trial testimony have different requirements. Subpoenas remain subject to relevant rules and limits.

A later response may affect current reporting or remaining loss. It does not automatically dispose of a claim concerning earlier actionable conduct. Identify the original duty, violation, causation, remedy and filing deadline rather than treating every late document as either a complete cure or permanently inadmissible.
