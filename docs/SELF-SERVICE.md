# Self-service and assistance

Recorded October 21, 2026. John Rigler's direction for Commons.

The purpose is to reduce unnecessary procedural burden: the opposite of
sludge. A reader should be able to walk the directory, understand the requested
action, adapt the relevant text, print it, and retain a complete copy without
buying a service or depending on a helper.

The public materials are freely reusable under the repository's CC0 dedication.
They provide procedural information rather than individual legal advice.
Free distribution describes this publishing model; it does not by itself settle
questions about legal advice, representation or authority to sign.

## Make the directory usable

- Start with the requested action. Link longer explanations from the terms.
- Provide ready-to-print PDFs beside editable HTML sources.
- Use numbered source parts, descriptive filenames and comments explaining roles.
- Keep personal evidence and identity material in the private case record.
- Preserve the exact completed version, signature record and actual dispatch history.
- Do not make login, payment, IPFS publication or a ledger transaction a prerequisite
  for reading, adapting or printing the reusable material.

## Make the helper's role visible

Preparing, approving, signing and sending are separate acts. The
[walkthrough and assistance record](../reading/assistance/) give each a place.
Someone can type, read aloud, assemble copies or mail a packet while the person
whose case it is remains the reviewer and signer. A helper signs their own name
for their own contribution.

Where someone actually signs on another person's behalf, record the actual signer,
capacity, separate authority, scope and date. The blank record neither grants
power of attorney nor establishes that the recipient will accept the authority.
Keep the person's approval tied to the identified version rather than every later edit.

## Source and scope

The CFPB's [guidance on submitting a complaint for another consumer](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/submitting-a-complaint-on-behalf-of-a-consumer/)
calls for identifying the helper and obtaining the consumer's permission;
companies may require written proof. Reviewed October 21, 2026. This source
concerns the CFPB complaint process. It is not a universal delegation rule for
credit reporting agencies or courts, and the Commons record is not an official form.

## Project placement

Commons publishes the reusable text and blank templates. Daisy records the dated
decision and individual case material within its access boundaries. SB Shell
assembles the publication. Mogwai supplies tools. CertLedger presents selected
case products and verification records; Chisel handles actual chain operations.
The self-service addition does not claim those later operations have occurred.

## Foundations and intersecting articles

Foundations organize broad subjects such as Real Estate and Religion. An article
can connect several foundations; Parsonage Exemption is the proposed example at
that intersection. Deterrence through administrative burden is a reusable article
that can support credit, traffic-ticket and VA cases. Cases can cite whichever
article editions apply. See [Foundations, articles and cases](FOUNDATIONS.md).
