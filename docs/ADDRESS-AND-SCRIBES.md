# The address, the stream and the scribes

John Rigler's architecture clarification. Recorded October 21, 2026.
“Byzantine scribes” is a tentative name, not a finalized protocol term.

## Commons is an address-based public stream

The shared address is the meeting point. Participants publish their own ledger
records associated with it. They can contribute articles, advertisements, cases,
offers, commentary or other material. Publication does not require admission
to this GitHub repository, selection by its maintainers, or acceptance into a
centrally approved subject list.

Foundations and article relationships are ways to organize and understand
material. They do not bound the stream. A record does not have to fit the
current catalog or templates to exist as a contribution.

This repository is one collection of documents and tooling for that wider
Commons. Its editable sources, reviewed editions and examples are useful
work products, not the authority that decides everything the address may carry.
Public-stream contributions do not change the access boundaries of the private
case evidence and identity packets maintained in the current projects.

## Independent servers provide independent views

A server operator can retrieve records associated with the address, preserve
copies or referenced content, build an index, provide search, and present
readable or printable views. Other operators can provide different views of
the same stream. A person can also use their own tools.

Each operator can describe what its view includes, how it classifies material,
what it checked, and what it omitted or has not retrieved. An operator's editorial
selection concerns that view. It does not determine whether an underlying ledger
record exists. Unknown record types can remain available for later interpretation.

This is the role Rigler tentatively calls **Byzantine scribes**: independent
keepers and readers of the record. Their accounts can be compared, and claims
about exact file bytes can be checked against the referenced content and
commitments. The term is a metaphor for the service role, not a newly specified
consensus algorithm. The ledger's consensus mechanism and the scribes'
interpretations remain separate.

“Scholars” could describe people who analyze and explain the material. “Scribes”
emphasizes recording, preserving, indexing and presenting it. Neither role
has exclusive authority over the public stream.

## Rigler.org is a service address, not a prerequisite

John owns **Rigler.org**, the .org domain matching his surname. His argument is
that other people should not need comparable ownership to participate. Owning
a useful domain is compatible with building a system that does not require one.

A domain can provide a memorable route to a scribe's server, teaching material
or other services. The underlying identity, publication and record references
should remain usable through other servers and local tools. A domain holder
should not acquire exclusive authority over a person's identity or the public
record merely by operating a convenient endpoint.

The intended naming model does not require everyone to compete for one globally
exclusive human-readable word. Readable labels can be interpreted in context;
the associated addresses, signing keys and exact record references provide ways
to distinguish what is being referenced. Those relationships must be explicit
where identity or provenance matters. This records a naming principle, not a
newly implemented naming protocol.

Rigler.org can be one doorway into the Commons. It need not be a boundary around
the Commons, and a change in that doorway should not redefine the underlying
records.

## People charge for the work they provide

| Participant | Potential paid work |
| --- | --- |
| Lawyers | Their ordinary legal work, augmented by the skill to understand and use these records. |
| John as a teacher | Teaching the system, its language, publishing methods and practical use. |
| Server operators | Hosting, preservation, indexing, search, retrieval and presentation. |
| Technical specialists | Implementing tools and examining files, signatures or ledger references. |
| Editors and researchers | Explaining, organizing, checking and developing material for a particular audience. |

These are examples rather than exclusive job categories. People can combine
roles. Fees compensate the service provided; operating a service does not
confer control over the Commons address or require everyone to use that service.

## One pool of meaning within a wider medium

Rigler calls the shared public medium the digital wilderness. Commons is one
project within it; game worlds, news, commerce and payment memos are other possible
pools of meaning. The common space can exist independently of a particular
provider's business. The [digital-wilderness note](DIGITAL-WILDERNESS.md) records
this distinction; the architecture connecting the projects is left for later.

## The language connects the participants

Readable records and references let people publish something another reader
can interpret. A participant can announce an article, advertise a service,
reference a case or connect subjects. A scribe supplies a view of those records;
a teacher explains their use; a professional applies relevant expertise.

The address supplies a shared place to look. The language makes the records
usable. Independent participants supply interpretation and services. That is
the connection to Rigler's internet of value.

This note records the model and naming discussion. It does not select a new
chain address, define a new message format, or claim a scribe service has been
deployed. Any operational announcement must identify the actual chain, address
and publication convention.
