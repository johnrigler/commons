# Professional competence, court use and paid expertise

John Rigler's project direction, with primary-source scope notes.
Recorded October 21, 2026.

Commons can provide a place where lawyers, technical specialists, courts and
the public develop a shared understanding of records in the internet of value.
Publicly reusable articles and paid professional work can reinforce one another.
The project aims to make professional participation productive within the
system, while keeping the underlying material independently accessible.

## A real competence principle, with a defined scope

ABA Model Rule 1.1 Comment 8 describes maintaining competence through awareness
of developments in practice, including the benefits and risks of relevant
technology, and continuing education. It does not require mastery of every new
product. The ABA text is a model; enforceable duties and consequences depend on
the jurisdiction's adopted rules and the circumstances. It is not a standalone
license-renewal rule requiring every lawyer to learn Commons or Web3. [1]

The project inference is that when ledger records, signatures and verifiable
editions matter to a representation, understanding them or obtaining appropriate
competent assistance may become part of doing the work properly. Commons can
support that understanding through inspectable examples, sources and explanations.
This is a proposed professional use, not official accreditation or CLE approval.

## Court understanding and court acceptance are different

Canon 3A(1) of the Code of Conduct for United States Judges addresses maintaining
professional competence in the law; Canon 3B(1) addresses judicial administration.
These are not mandates to adopt a named platform or endorse an internet-of-value
theory. Rigler's argument is that useful professional understanding will become
increasingly important as relevant records come before courts. That is a project
thesis and forecast, not an established universal adoption requirement. [2]

Federal Rule of Evidence 901 supplies an authentication standard and examples,
including evidence about a process or system. Rules 902(13) and (14) provide
routes for self-authentication of certain electronic records or copied data
with qualified-person certification and notice requirements. [3]

These rules supply existing ways to assess digital evidence, not automatic
admissibility for every blockchain reference. Relevance, hearsay, privilege,
expertise where required and other applicable requirements remain separate.
The governing court's rules control; the federal examples are not a statement
of every state or tribunal's procedure.

Commons' practical opportunity is to make the claim and verification method
clear enough to examine: what file was retained, how it was identified, what
the signing key establishes, what the ledger records, and what remains disputed.
A demonstration should make its checks reproducible without overstating their
legal effect. No court approval of Commons is asserted.

## Expertise people can pay for

Shared text can give a client and professional a useful starting point. There
is still substantial work in applying it responsibly to a particular matter.

| Professional contribution | Work performed |
| --- | --- |
| Legal review of an article | Check sources, jurisdiction, current law, scope and exceptions; identify the edition reviewed. |
| Advice on an individual case | Evaluate facts, missing evidence, available options and the requested relief. |
| Record and provenance analysis | Examine original files, signatures, timestamps, ledger references and the limits of each result, with technical assistance where needed. |
| Court preparation | Develop the evidentiary foundation, declarations or expert material where appropriate, and address objections. |
| Advocacy and resolution | Negotiate, draft case-specific arguments and represent the client. |
| Professional education | Teach methods, explain examples and train practitioners; approved CLE status would require the applicable approval process. |

These are potential service roles, not a claim that every task requires a
lawyer, that every participant is qualified, or that payment is a condition of
reading or using Commons. Fee arrangements remain subject to the applicable
rules. ABA Model Rule 1.5 addresses reasonable fees and communication of their
basis; it is a model reference, not a universal fee authorization. [4]

## How this fits the project story

Commons shifts professional value toward application, evaluation, accountability
and advocacy. Making a basic explanation public can reduce repeated explanatory
work while creating opportunities to improve articles and serve individual clients.

The design removes mandatory dependence on one intermediary. Professionals can
offer services within that design without owning the only route to the shared
knowledge. Readers can use a public article independently, seek paid assistance,
or consult another professional.
