# Foundations, articles and cases

Recorded October 21, 2026. John Rigler's Commons architecture direction.

**Foundations are broad subjects or organizing concerns. Articles are the
reusable documents that develop a particular issue.** An article can connect
multiple foundations. A case can reference multiple articles. Commons is a
network of these relationships rather than a directory tree in which every
article has exactly one parent.

## These are relationships within an open stream

Foundations, articles and cases describe one way to read and organize the
Commons. They are not mandatory admission categories. Anyone can publish
articles, advertisements, cases or other records associated with the shared
address without submitting them to this repository. The repository's catalog
and individual server views select and interpret material within that wider
stream. See [the address and the scribes](ADDRESS-AND-SCRIBES.md).

## Open subject scope

Commons is a vessel for reusable explanations, arguments and practical material
wherever they make sense. Its scope is not limited to credit disputes, legal
process, the first contributors' experience, or a fixed list of foundations.

The subject areas discussed so far include:

| Foundation or subject area | Place in Commons |
| --- | --- |
| Finance and credit | One substantial universe of related articles and cases. |
| Healthcare | An independent subject area, with connections where relevant. |
| Religion | Its own subject area; articles may intersect with other foundations. |
| Real Estate | Its own subject area; may connect with religion, finance or other concerns. |
| Investing | Another possible subject area, not restricted to credit disputes. |
| Felony proceedings and fraud | Included as possible subject matter even though John has not claimed personal experience in these areas. |
| Further subjects | Develop where contributors bring useful material and readers find a reason to use it. |

The initial author's experience provides starting material, not a boundary on
what the Commons may contain. New contributors can bring knowledge and sources
for areas the original author does not know. Articles can cross subjects without
requiring those subjects to share one root argument.

## Development follows use

Participation, reader interest and reuse should shape which articles and subject
areas grow and receive attention. The original author need not predetermine the
most important topic. Popularity can shape prominence; supporting sources,
authorship, scope and review status remain separate ways to assess reliability.

This records the intended direction. It does not claim that voting, popularity
ranking or subject filters are already implemented in this repository. Public
ledger publication is distinct from submission to a repository or server.

## The example that defines the model

Real Estate and Religion can be separate foundations. A proposed **Parsonage
Exemption** article connects both. Its placement does not require deriving it
from the administrative-burden document. This is an organizational example;
no substantive exemption rule or tax guidance has been drafted or verified.

| Article | Foundations or concerns it can connect | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deterrence through administrative burden | Procedural burden across institutions and subject areas. | Reusable explanation in the current reading edition. |
| Unsupported rent acceleration | Real Estate and procedural burden; credit-reporting consequences where relevant. | Reusable reading draft exists; individual evidence remains separate. |
| Parsonage Exemption | Real Estate and Religion. | Proposed intersection article; scope and sources not yet selected. |
| Traffic tickets and insurance consequences | Traffic enforcement, insurance and procedural burden. | Proposed sludge case/article area; John's documents and jurisdiction not yet supplied. |
| Veterans dealing with the VA | Veterans' affairs and procedural burden. | Proposed case/article area; identify the particular decision and procedure. |

These connections describe relevance, not a finding about an individual case.
An article does not have to link to every foundation. A foundation may have its
own introduction without being identical to one fixed document.

## The existing burden document

**Deterrence through administrative burden** is the formal title of the document
John has referred to as the sludge document. Sludge is the shorter term for
excessive or unjustified procedural friction. It can support articles or cases
about credit reporting, traffic tickets or VA processes without becoming the
single root of all Commons material.

The traffic-ticket inquiry examines which burdens discourage a challenge and
how record or insurance consequences depend on resources. Arizona diversion in
the [reading companion](../reading/assistance/) documents one mechanism; it does
not identify John's case or establish uniform purposes or rules for traffic
systems. Insurers' downstream use of risk information differs from credit
bureaus' record-maintenance and distribution role.

## Articles and individual cases

An article explains a reusable issue and identifies its scope and sources. An
individual case adds the actual chronology, evidence, questions and requested
action. The case may cite several exact article editions where relevant.

The religious-exemption subject is another possible article area within Religion,
with scope and sources still to be selected. It need not connect to the burden
article. Parsonage Exemption illustrates an intersection, not a new legal claim.

## Publication structure

Keep articles identifiable by title, author, scope, sources and exact edition.
Retain the established numbered source parts and immutable snapshots. Existing
HTML articles remain the concrete documents. This note records the relationship
model; it does not claim a topic-filtering interface or new catalog schema has
already been implemented.

## Why this structure matters

[Commons and the internet of value](COMMONS-AND-WEB3.md) develops the project
story: reusable reasoning can accumulate across foundations and cases, retain
its sources and versions, and reduce dependence on repeatedly purchased
explanations. This is an economic and editorial hypothesis, not a finding about
the motives of the legal profession.
