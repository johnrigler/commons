# Digital wilderness and pools of meaning

John Rigler's project direction, October 21, 2026.
Editorial note with a bounded Texas statutory comparison.

## The public space can exist independently of one provider

Rigler calls the shared public medium the **digital wilderness**. Within it,
people can establish pools of meaning: Commons, a game world, a news feed,
commerce, or a memo associated with a payment. Each is a project or use of the
medium, not the whole medium.

Commons is one such project. Its existence need not depend on one business
continuing to operate a particular service. People can contribute records,
read them through different tools and build different services around them.

This note captures that distinction. It does not specify the wider architecture,
a new message format or a new deployment.

## Why the comparison starts with Satoshi

The Bitcoin white paper's introduction describes how trusted financial
intermediation and dispute mediation introduce costs and constraints. Its
proposed payment system addresses double-spending without requiring that trusted
intermediary. [1]

Rigler extends that argument to institutional obligations. A provider may acquire
duties, costs and exposure through the role it occupies, even when the people
operating it would prefer a simpler relationship. He uses KYC and
anti-money-laundering requirements as examples in his comparison. This is his
extension; it is not presented as the paper's stated KYC/AML thesis.

The link to sludge concerns the costs imposed on participation. A justified
requirement can impose work without being excessive or unjustified. Whether
a particular process constitutes sludge needs its own examination.

## The service business and the common space

Rigler describes a tension: providing help through a business may require
resources for operation, compliance and defense. Those costs can create pressure
to charge for something he would also like people to be able to share freely.

That is an economic concern, not a conclusion that he is legally required to
form a business or charge fees. The common record and a business offering
individual services around it are different things. Teachers, lawyers, server
operators and other participants can provide their own services without becoming
the owner of the shared space.

The digital-wilderness model concerns that independence. It does not exempt
participants from the rules applicable to their own conduct.

## The Texas recollection: keep the distinctions

Government Code §81.101 defines practice by activities, not a general fee test.
Subsection (c) excludes specified publication and sale of materials and software
with a clear, conspicuous statement that they are not a substitute for attorney
advice; Chapter 83 remains applicable. Section 81.102 addresses authority to
practice. Forming a business supplies no law license. [2]

Penal Code §§38.122–38.123 include economic-benefit elements for distinct offenses.
Chapter 83 separately restricts compensated preparation of certain real-property
instruments. These may connect to Rigler's recollection; the old book and passage
have not been identified. Free versus paid alone does not settle the issue. [3][4]

This is Texas statutory context, not an assessment of a particular service's
legality or a claim that Texas rules govern every Commons participant.

## Where the observation belongs

Keep the distinction between an independent public Commons and particular
providers in the Commons story. Broader worldbuilding around game worlds,
news, commerce and other pools of meaning can be developed with their respective
projects. The architecture connecting them is left aside for now, as requested.
