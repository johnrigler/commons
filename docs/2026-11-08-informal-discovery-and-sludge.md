# Informal discovery, procedural sludge, and the cost of representation

*Commons research / editorial note · 8 November 2026 · not a court finding or signed edition*

## Core argument

The cost of challenging an asserted debt is part of the system in which the debt is collected. An institution may be able to issue, forward, and maintain a claim through routine operations, while the person disputing it incurs repeated learning, compliance, time, and potentially legal fees to obtain an answer about the claim's basis. This is **procedural sludge** or **asymmetric structural friction**. Its consequences can be examined without assuming that every participant acts with fraudulent intent.

The question is not only whether a claimant can eventually win in court. It is whether an unsupported claim can remain economically collectible because verifying or contesting it is expensive enough that payment or abandonment becomes attractive.

## Direct calls as informal discovery

A person who is not represented by counsel can initiate calls to ask a collector about the collector's own procedures, what dispute was logged, which records were considered, who made the decision, and how a disagreement reaches a manager. Recording those exchanges where lawful can document the process and the varying answers given. This is **informal discovery**, not court discovery: it does not compel evidence production, complete responses, or testimony under oath.

Representation changes the ordinary communication channel. Debt collectors face restrictions on communicating directly with consumers known to be represented by counsel concerning the debt, with statutory exceptions (15 U.S.C. §1692c(a)(2)). That does not create a general rule that only a represented person may speak to a manager. A specific access barrier must be established from the actual call or written policy. An attorney can supply substantial protection and legally enforceable discovery, but representation can also add expense and mediate direct access to the staff who operate the process.

## The professional ecosystem

An attorney acting for a consumer and a collection agency may be adversaries in a case but repeat professional participants in the same legal ecosystem. They share procedural expectations, courts, and professional channels. The consumer may be a one-time participant. Competent, ethical representation does not erase this structural difference.

There can be a **principal-agent tension** between the client's goals and the professional's work: counsel may reasonably focus on obtaining settlement, correction, deletion, or dismissal, while the client may also seek to understand and expose the process that generated the disputed claim. This is not evidence of collusion between counsel and collectors, nor a reason to reject legal help categorically.

## The verification-loop hypothesis

A useful investigation asks what happens after a consumer challenges a creditor's calculation rather than simply denying identity or receipt of a bill:

1. Is the precise objection preserved?
2. Which source records are collected and evaluated, and by whom?
3. Is a creditor's confirmation treated as conclusive, or is there a defined path for rejecting it?
4. Can the collector identify what evidence would change the result?
5. How many contacts, transfers, fees, and days elapse before a substantive answer?
6. Does attorney status alter access to escalation, and if so, under what actual policy?

These are falsifiable questions about a procedure. An account of one disputed debt may suggest a systemic vulnerability, but it does not by itself prove an organization designed its process to promote fraud, that an employee committed fraud, or that every debt referred through that process is inaccurate.

## Record and publication boundaries

Preserve the original audio, machine transcript, checked transcript, and any public redacted derivative as **separate byte-identical artifacts**, each with its own digest and provenance. A source record may be private while a public argument discusses the general mechanism. Do not expose account identifiers, verification answers, other tenants, or unreviewed voice recordings in this public note.

A SHA-256 digest, CID, signature, or Litecoin announcement establishes an exact-version reference, **not** the truth of an allegation, a legal determination, or authorization to release the recording. Do not imply a signature or on-chain event before it occurs.
